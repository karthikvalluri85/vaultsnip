VaultSnip privacy policy

Short version: VaultSnip stores nothing about you or your images.

  • No accounts, no backend, no database. VaultSnip is static code that runs in your browser.
  • Your images stay on your device. Text recognition (OCR), masking and the safety check run locally in your browser. Images and results are held in memory and disappear when you close the tab.
  • What leaves your device. Only when you click send, and only the masked image you approved, goes directly from your browser to the AI provider you chose, Anthropic (Claude) or OpenAI, using your own API key. It never passes through a VaultSnip server. That provider's API terms and data policy apply to the request.
  • Numbers in the masked image are invented. By default each number (amounts, percentages, scores, temperatures, counts) is replaced with a different, made-up value of the same size: $4.2M might show as $5.6M. The real values never leave your device, but the rough size of each number does (same number of digits, same unit). Names, labels, emails and IDs stay covered by grey boxes, and the image carries a note saying its numbers are made up. Switch it off under Redaction policy to cover numbers with grey boxes instead.
  • Customer comments (optional). If you tick Keep the wording of comments, the ordinary words of quotes and free-text feedback stay readable in the image you send, and can appear in the replica and its export. Names, companies, emails, numbers and IDs inside them stay masked, and the replica only quotes words that were readable in the image you approved. It is off by default.
  • Samples send nothing at all.
  • Build on this device (beta). Instead of sending the masked image, you can have VaultSnip read the layout itself. It runs in your browser (a background worker on this device), uses only the masked image and the words that stay readable in it (masked words reach it as positions, never as text), and sends nothing. It needs no key, downloads nothing new and stores nothing. The replica it builds follows the same privacy modes, exports and share-link rules as any other.
  • On-device name check (on by default). A small AI model looks for safe words used as names ("May Chen", "Delta Airlines") and adds masks for them. It runs in your browser, on this device; the text it reads never leaves it. The model is an app file (19 MB) served by VaultSnip itself, never by a third party; extension packages built for the stores include it. It only adds masks, and you can switch it off under Redaction policy.
  • Unread-text check (on by default). A second small AI model finds every patch of text in the screenshot without reading it, so text the reader missed (tiny, rotated or faint labels) is masked too; a third reads such a patch once more, one line at a time, to tell a safe label from a name. They run in your browser, on this device, and see only the image's pixels; nothing leaves the device. The models are app files (5 MB and 7 MB) served by VaultSnip itself, never by a third party; extension packages built for the stores include them. It only adds masks, and you can switch it off under Redaction policy.
  • What your browser keeps. The OCR language model (an app file, cached so it downloads once) and, only if you tick "remember", your API key, its provider and the model name. You can remove them at any time from "AI key" → "Forget key". "Check key" asks the provider which models the key can use; it sends only the key.
  • Blur text in charts (extension). "Blur text in charts on this page" (right-click menu, or Alt+Shift+B) reads the charts drawn on that page, finds sensitive text on your device (the same OCR, rules and unread-text check as the app, with the same models packed in the extension), and blurs it in place. Nothing is sent or stored, and running it again removes the blur. It runs only on the tab where you choose it.
  • Extension permissions. No access to any website until you act on a tab, and none afterwards. activeTab: capture or blur the tab you are on, only when you click the icon, the menu item or the shortcut. scripting: put the chart blur into that tab when you ask for it. offscreen: a hidden extension page that reads the chart text on your device. contextMenus: the "Blur text in charts on this page" menu item. None of them shows a warning when you install.
  • Downloads (the masked PNG, the CSV of generated data) are made in your browser and saved to your computer; nothing is sent.
  • Share links hold the replica inside the link itself (the part after #, which browsers never send to a server). VaultSnip uploads and stores nothing. A link carries only what the level you pick shows: titles, labels and chart shapes (Shape-preserving), titles and labels (Structure-only) or the layout alone (Generic). It never carries your screenshot, the masked image or any real value. Anyone you give the link to can open it.
  • Realistic names. With Realistic names on (Shape-preserving and Structure-only modes), the replica fills names, companies, emails, IDs and covered labels such as "[Client]" with made-up values from Faker's name lists, chosen on your device from each column's name, the dashboard's country or currency, and the industry its visible words point to (healthcare, finance, retail…). Organisation names are invented in that industry's style and checked against a list of well-known real organisations, which are never produced. They are not taken from your screenshot. Emails use only the reserved example.com, example.net and example.org domains and phone numbers only ranges set aside for fiction, so no generated contact detail can reach a real person. Nothing is downloaded or stored for this. Switch it off to see labels named from their column or dimension instead, such as "Owner A" or "Region C"; Generic mode never uses it. Either way, a label the replica cannot read (an axis or legend label that is covered) is filled from fixed lists chosen by its dimension and the dashboard: months for a time axis, regions, segments, channels and the like, never from the masked text.
  • Exports are files saved to your computer. They contain generated data, a watermark and a link to VaultSnip, and only the labels their privacy mode shows. They make no network calls by themselves.
  • Design exports. Export for Figma (SVG) saves one SVG drawing of the replica (text, shapes, chart vectors and the SYNTHETIC DATA watermark). Export React code saves a zip, either an editable TypeScript project (one typed component per card, a hook and types) or a one-file component, with the VaultSnip replica engine, the replica's spec and the map outlines it uses; the component generates its values when it runs. Both are made in your browser from the replica in its current privacy mode (a Generic export holds no original titles or labels, not even in hidden fields) and saved to your computer; nothing is sent or stored.
  • Feedback is your choice. The Feedback button prepares a report (VaultSnip version, browser, the step you were on, chart types and any error message, never your screenshot or dashboard text). Nothing is sent until you choose to continue on GitHub, where it is posted under your GitHub account, or to email it from your own mail app. GitHub's or your email provider's terms apply.
  • Developer benchmarks are not part of the app. The accuracy tools in tools/ run only when a developer starts them, or in this project's CI. They draw fictional dashboards from invented data, and download a small set of public dashboard images (from Microsoft Learn, Google Cloud documentation and Tableau Public) to score against; nothing of yours is involved, and the app and extension never run them. One of them, tools/ai-bench, measures the AI-key path: it sends only those invented dashboards, masked, to the AI provider with the developer's own key, read from their environment; the public dashboard images are never sent.
  • No analytics, cookies or tracking.
